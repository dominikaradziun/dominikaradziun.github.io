I am a computational cognitive neuroscientist studying how the brain builds models of the self and the world, and how these models adapt when sensory experience changes.

I am currently a FNRS Postdoctoral Researcher/Chargée de recherche FNRS at the [Crossmodal Perception and Plasticity Lab](https://cpplab.be/) at UCLouvain. My research asks why congenital blindness may protect against schizophrenia, and what this unusual relationship can teach us about resilience and vulnerability in mental health. More broadly, I am interested in how sensory experience shapes the brain’s internal models and how differences in these models may contribute to both adaptive and maladaptive outcomes.

Previously, I was a Postdoctoral Researcher at the [Brain, Body, and Technology Lab](https://www.brainbodytech.com/) at the Donders Institute, Radboud University. I completed my PhD at Karolinska Institutet, where I investigated body perception and brain plasticity in blind and sighted individuals. My doctoral thesis was awarded the [Glushko Dissertation Prize](https://cognitivesciencesociety.org/glushko-dissertation-prize/) from the Cognitive Science Society. You can watch my Glushko Prize talk [here](https://underline.io/events/465/sessions/18086/lecture/99279-body-perception-and-brain-plasticity-in-blind-and-sighted-individuals).

Originally from Poland, my academic journey has taken me through Norway, Sweden, the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, and now Belgium. As a scientist, I feel privileged to ask big questions and to mentor students as they grow into researchers themselves.

I am especially happy to hear from students and early-career researchers interested in cognitive neuroscience, brain plasticity, blindness, body perception, or mental health. If you are considering research in these areas, navigating an academic transition, or simply have a question about my work, please feel free to reach out by email.
